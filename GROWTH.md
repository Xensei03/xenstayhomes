# Xenstay Homes — SEO, imagery & content roadmap

Everything here is strategy, not code. The site itself is `index.html`.

---

## 1. Keyword map

You are a **local service business**. You will not rank for "airbnb management" (national,
dominated by Vacasa/Evolve/AvantStay). You *can* own "airbnb management sacramento" and its
long tail — far less volume, far more intent, and the searcher is 20 minutes from your office.

### Primary — the money terms

| Keyword | Intent | Where it lives now |
|---|---|---|
| airbnb management sacramento | Commercial | Title, H1 region, schema |
| short term rental management sacramento | Commercial | Meta, body |
| airbnb property management roseville / folsom / auburn | Commercial | Needs its own page |
| airbnb co-host sacramento | Commercial | Needs body copy |
| vacation rental management northern california | Commercial | Meta, body |
| property management sacramento 10% | Comparison | Owned outright — nobody else can claim it |

### Long tail — where you'll actually win first

These have low competition and high conversion. Each deserves a blog post that links to the
home page with a keyword-rich anchor:

- "how much do airbnb property managers charge in sacramento"
- "is airbnb management worth it sacramento"
- "sacramento short term rental rules / permit requirements"
- "roseville airbnb regulations"
- "airbnb vs long term rental sacramento which earns more"
- "how much can i make on airbnb in sacramento"
- "best airbnb management companies sacramento" *(you want to be in the comparison set)*
- "how to switch airbnb property managers"

### The strategic gap

Your fee is the single most searchable differentiator you have. "How much do airbnb managers
charge" is a high-volume, high-intent query that every competitor answers evasively because
their answer is bad. **Answer it directly, in public, with a number.** A page titled
*"What Airbnb Management Costs in Sacramento (2026 Rates, Compared)"* that names real fee
ranges will earn links and rank — and every visitor who reads it discovers you're the cheapest
credible option on the page.

## 2. Local SEO — do these in order

1. **Google Business Profile.** Non-negotiable. You currently have no phone number on the
   site and no GBP. This is worth more than every other item on this list combined. Category:
   "Property management company". Add photos, service areas, and get review requests going.
2. **NAP consistency.** Name/Address/Phone identical on the site, GBP, and every directory.
   The site now emits it in schema — make it match exactly.
3. **Reviews.** Ask your 28 owners. Ten Google reviews will move you more than any on-page work.
4. **City pages.** One per market, 600+ words of genuinely local content — permit rules, demand
   drivers, what a 3BR earns there. Not spun templates; Google detects those. Start with
   Roseville, Auburn and Folsom (highest ADR, least competition).
5. **Citations.** Yelp, Nextdoor, BBB, local chambers.

## 4. AI image prompts

The current photos are cropped Airbnb screenshots. They work, but they're capped at ~1100px
of real detail and compressed twice. Below are prompts for generating supporting imagery —
**use these for atmosphere and process shots, never to misrepresent a property you manage.**
Real photography of real homes always wins for the portfolio grid.

### Hero / atmosphere

> Golden-hour architectural photograph of a modern California ranch home in the Sierra
> foothills, wide shot from a low three-quarter angle. Shot on a Sony A7R IV, 24mm tilt-shift
> lens at f/8, vertical lines perfectly corrected. Warm low sun raking across a stone facade
> and oak trees, long soft shadows, haze in the valley behind. Palette of warm cream, aged
> bronze, deep forest green and charcoal. Editorial architectural-digest mood, calm and
> expensive, no people, no signage. Ultra-sharp, natural colour, 8K.

### Owner / trust

> Editorial portrait of a relaxed homeowner in their forties on a sunlit patio, laptop closed
> beside a coffee cup, looking off-camera with a slight smile. Shot on Canon R5, 85mm at f/1.4,
> creamy background separation. Soft late-afternoon window light from camera left, warm skin
> tones, natural texture, no retouching gloss. Neutral linen wardrobe, warm cream and sage
> palette. Documentary lifestyle mood — ease, not celebration. 8K.

### Housekeeping / operations

> Documentary photograph of a professional housekeeping team finishing a turnover in a bright
> Californian living room, mid-motion, smoothing a white linen bedspread. Shot on Fujifilm
> GFX100 with a 45mm lens at f/4. Bright diffused daylight through sheer curtains, clean whites
> with warm undertones, visible fabric texture. Candid, competent, unposed — hospitality-brand
> documentary style, not stock photography. Muted palette, natural colour, 8K.

### Maintenance

> Close photograph of a tradesperson's hands adjusting a brass fixture in a well-appointed
> bathroom, shallow depth of field. Shot on Sony A7 IV, 90mm macro at f/2.8. Warm directional
> light, brushed brass against pale stone, water droplets catching light. Craft and care as the
> subject. Warm neutral palette with bronze accents. Editorial, tactile, 8K.

### Owner dashboard / reporting

> Overhead flat-lay of a matte-black tablet displaying a clean financial dashboard with warm
> bronze accent charts, on a pale oak desk. Beside it: a linen notebook, brass pen, ceramic
> coffee cup, single eucalyptus sprig. Shot on Canon R5, 50mm at f/5.6, soft diffused overhead
> light, gentle shadows. Cream, oak, bronze and forest-green palette. Minimal, premium,
> Kinfolk-magazine styling. 8K.

### Local lifestyle (for city pages)

> Wide golden-hour photograph of the American River bike trail near Folsom, California, in
> early autumn. Shot on Sony A7R IV, 35mm at f/5.6. Warm backlight through oak and cottonwood,
> lens flare, distant cyclists as small silhouettes. Californian golden-brown grass, deep green
> river, warm haze. Aspirational travel-editorial mood, no identifiable faces. 8K.

**Consistency rule:** every prompt shares the same palette — *warm cream, aged bronze, deep
forest green, charcoal* — and the same lighting character: *warm, low, directional, natural*.
That's what makes a set of images read as one brand instead of a mood board.

## 6. Two things to be careful about

**Don't add review schema until the reviews are real and public.** The site deliberately omits
`aggregateRating` from its structured data. Marking up anonymous on-page testimonials as
review data is a manual-action risk with Google, and the upside (a star in the SERP) is not
worth a penalty. Once you have real Google reviews, they'll show anyway.

**Keep the calculator honest.** The nightly rates in it are three-bedroom-equivalent medians
derived from your own portfolio, and the default occupancy is deliberately conservative at 165
nights. Resist the temptation to raise those numbers — an owner who is promised $60K and earns
$38K is a refund request and a bad review, and income projections are a regulated grey area.
The fee comparison is the persuasive part, and it's pure arithmetic that cannot be wrong.
